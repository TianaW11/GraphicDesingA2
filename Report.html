Campaign Report

Introduction to selected UNSDG: 

The UN Sustainable Development Goal I selected is Goal 11, which includes target 11.7. The main goal is to make cities and human settlements inclusive, safe, resilient and sustainable. Target 11.7 specifically calls for providing universal access to safe, inclusive, and accessible green and public spaces by 2023, particularly for women and children, older persons, and persons with disabilities. Some issues include the scarcity of green spaces, the marginalization of those who have access, and a lack of maintenance and sustainability. 

Target audience:

My target audience is broad, as my message is that everyone should hear about and care about this issue because everyone deserves to enjoy the benefits of green spaces. However, it is limited to Canada, as my data comes only from Canadian cities. Age-wise, because my main sources are an online video and a webpage, this may exclude older adults who don’t use technology as much as younger generations. With that in mind, my audience would be those who use the web and have a presence on social media.  For distribution channels, since my campaign centers on Park People's data collection, I am thinking of a partnership between my campaign and their organization. So they would sell the physical seed packet online through their donation section: if you donate a certain amount, you receive a seed packet. Another option is that when Park People hosts in-person events, they can sell the packet. Obviously, all sales will go to the organization and towards the issues my campaign is addressing. For the webpage itself, I will post my video on social media platforms and include the webpage link in the description. Viewers who want to learn more can click the link to see the rest of my campaign and support the organization I linked at the bottom. Giving people a way to keep learning after the video provides a path to understand the issue and what actions they can take. It doesn’t stop at knowledge; it moves to a simple action anyone can take, like planting a seed. My main personal goal is to remind people of the importance of nature and the space it naturally creates for us, putting viewers in the scene of what we’ll lose if we don’t take action. It also highlights the UN goal and the local organizations making real change. 

Design Justification: 

My major outputs are the video and webpage. My goal is to reach as many people as possible because this campaign is supposed to benefit everyone, as green spaces are for everyone. A social media post reaches a wider range of viewers, while the webpage serves as a home page that anyone with access to a device can use. For visual purposes, I created this page so I could use different techniques and create different visuals. I thought including elements I made would add a human touch and an organic feel of nature. This inspired all the visual elements I used. For the video, I used background clips from when I walked the trails to place viewers in nature and remind them of its beauty and why it should be preserved. I kept the typography as a sans-serif for easy readability, playing with size and colour for emphasis. For colour, I chose green and orange, inspired by the UN SDG logo and nature. The colour worked well for the background video because of the fall season. On the webpage, I originally used a flat green background, then for the final version I added a gradient mimicking the flower’s transition. I drew the flower dying in three drawings to show the progression of the impact the survey results have on nature. Leading down to the pot, displaying a visual of the survey results. For the title typeface, I used a typeface with changing stroke weight and movement, like plants, to make the main title more pronounced, since it carries the main message. Finally, for the seed packet design, I used the same approach as the rest of the campaign to tie them together. The hand-drawn orange-and-green design conveys the same message as the webpage title. I also used an environmentally friendly, trademarked paper texture. 

Reflection: 

After learning about the issues and reading the survey results, and seeing Canada take on the 30 by 30 UN goal, I realized it’s up to us to make a change. These issues stem from people at all levels, from those who use the space most to those with the most authority over it. All levels in our society are responsible for preserving green spaces, which means providing access for everyone and maintaining teh space because it benefits everyone. When I learned that residents don’t feel like they have a voice, I realized I needed to use my own voice and bring people’s attention to take action where they can. Providing my site acts as a hub for where people want to go next. What I find with the UN goals is that they don’t provide actionable goals for people who don’t hold powerful positions. The best way to tackle an issue is to start from the bottom: start with those who care most and give them the resources and information to motivate them to solve the issue or participate in making a positive change. The reading that impacted my decisions most was the research paper on affective learning objectives for communication. It touches on pathos and intent. It discusses how to evoke emotion and humanize data. I felt like it related to my goal. Since the data comes from people's feelings, it uses pathos to appeal to emotions and what people felt based on the survey. It uses words that people find personal and advocates for that personal voice. Using this to drive a deeper cognitive or affective outcome. For sensory elements, providing an immersive experience to evoke emotional engagement, further pushing the intent to support the cause and take action. This helped me focus on visuals that move and are staged to evoke emotion, like the wilting flower on the page, which makes viewers feel something is wrong and encourages them to learn what's wrong and how they can help. Overall, this project helped me think about different forms of visual elements.
